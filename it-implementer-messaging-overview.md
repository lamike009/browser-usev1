# What IT implementers put on the homepage

Captured 7 October 2026. This is the companion to the vendor scan. That one read Salesforce, HubSpot, Cloudflare, Zendesk, and Atlassian. This one reads the firms a company hires to install those products and run the rollout.

The row-level record is in `it-implementer-messaging.csv`.

## Who is in the thirty

The pool was implementation consultancies and platform partners, not the software vendors. US Google traffic, organic and paid, was estimated for 114 of those domains. The thirty below are the ones a buyer actually lands on when they want someone to come in and plug the system in.

Two kinds of firm made the cut, because both do the work and both get found:

- **Global integrators and digital consultancies** with the search traffic: Accenture, Cognizant, Infosys, Persistent, Thoughtworks, EPAM, Capgemini, Wipro, Slalom, Globant, Publicis Sapient, West Monroe, Bounteous, Merkle, Valtech, DEPT.
- **Specialist partners** whose business is the installation: IMPACT, Bluleadz, Weidert, New Breed, Ironpaper, and SmartBug (HubSpot and revenue systems), INSIDEA, Fast Slow Motion, and Aptitude 8 (HubSpot, and in Fast Slow Motion’s case Salesforce and Claude too), Adaptavist, Deviniti, and Praecipio (Atlassian), Faye (CRM and CX deployment, including Zendesk, Salesforce, and HubSpot), and Coastal Cloud (Salesforce).

PwC, Deloitte, EY, and KPMG outrank every name on this list on US Google traffic. PwC’s organic estimate is about 1.5 million visits a month, against about 246,000 for Accenture. Their homepages are audit and professional-services conglomerates, so they are not in the thirty. A buyer looking for “come install HubSpot” does not get that sentence from those front doors.

Paid search is almost absent. Across these thirty, the paid-traffic estimates are effectively zero except Accenture (74), Cognizant (35), and Praecipio (14). The vendors in the earlier scan buy the click. The implementers wait for a conversation.

## The promise that is getting shared

The specialists and the global firms are not saying the same thing.

**Specialists: the software you bought is not the result. We are.**

- Bluleadz: “Fix your go-to-market strategy. Power it with HubSpot and AI.” The next line is the job: the strategy is stuck in a slide deck and the CRM is duct tape.
- New Breed: activate HubSpot’s customer platform, with services, agents, and apps.
- SmartBug: full-lifecycle systems “fueled by HubSpot,” ending in “Grow.”
- Fast Slow Motion: “We help businesses use Salesforce, HubSpot, and Claude.”
- Coastal Cloud: “help organizations do more with Salesforce and data.”
- Faye: “We eat complexity.” The rotating words are software, AI, integrations, custom development, and complexity. The offer is deployment plus managed services.
- Aptitude 8: HubSpot as the center of the go-to-market stack. They say outright that they are not a marketing agency.
- Praecipio: “Claude + Atlassian,” from North America’s largest pure-play Atlassian partner.
- Valtech, on a larger agency site, has the same idea in different clothes: “Your platforms, your data, your people. Renew, not re-platform.”

**Global firms: we turn AI ambition into something that runs.**

- Cognizant: “We’re an AI Builder. Bridging the gap from AI to impact.”
- Thoughtworks: “We don’t just do AI. We do AI that works.”
- Globant: “AI agents that execute. Domain experts who supervise.”
- Wipro: “Billions in AI spend, but stuck in pilot phase?”
- Slalom: “The ambition-to-execution gap is widening.”
- Accenture’s standing line is “Let there be change” and “Together, we can reinvent anything.” Salesforce is not in the hero.

The sentence that travels inside the specialist set is: **we configure the platform you already chose, and we stay until it runs the business.** The sentence that travels inside the integrator set is: **AI is stuck, and our people will get it into production.** The platform name is how the specialists prove they are the right people. The global firms treat the platform as a detail.

## Where the promise is delivered

On a vendor site, the next screen is a product module and a free trial. On an implementer site, the next screen is proof that humans have done this before, and the button opens a conversation.

**Specialist pattern.** Platform in the headline or the first sentence. A partner badge immediately after: HubSpot Elite, Platinum, Partner of the Year, certification counts, a review score. Then a service menu: implementation, migration, integration, training, managed service. Then one button.

| Firm | What the button says |
| --- | --- |
| Bluleadz | Talk to a strategist |
| INSIDEA | Book a strategy call |
| Fast Slow Motion | Let's talk |
| Faye | Talk to an expert now |
| New Breed | Get in touch |
| Praecipio | Schedule a consultation today |
| IMPACT | Book a call to see if we're a fit |
| Aptitude 8 | Get started |
| Coastal Cloud | Learn more about us |

Nobody publishes a price. Nobody offers a free trial. IMPACT adds a free book chapter, which is the specialist version of a lead magnet. Bluleadz and Ironpaper ask the visitor to pick a problem before the form. That diagnostic is the closest thing this set has to the vendor’s “start free.”

**Integrator pattern.** The homepage is a newsroom, a research carousel, or a case-study row. Accenture, Capgemini, Wipro, Infosys, Persistent, and Merkle lead with a point of view or a report. The client logo and one outcome number do the proof: Bacardi’s 16x deployment capacity on EPAM, Party City’s Shopify move on Merkle, McDonald’s on Accenture. Contact us lives in the nav or at the bottom. The Salesforce, HubSpot, or Zendesk practice is a page you still have to find.

**A few firms productize the service.** Globant sells AI Pods and talks about output-based pricing. DEPT leads with DEPTIFY, an orchestration layer. Publicis Sapient leads with Slingshot, Bodhi, and Sustain. Thoughtworks leads with AI/works. That is the integrator borrowing the vendor’s move: name a thing you own, then wrap people around it.

## The pain points being solved

1. **The license is not the system.** Bluleadz’s duct-tape CRM, New Breed’s un-activated HubSpot, Aptitude 8’s stack that still does not run revenue, Coastal’s Salesforce that should be doing more. This is the pain the specialist owns.
2. **AI spend is stuck in pilot.** Wipro, Merkle’s playbook, Slalom’s ambition-to-execution gap, Cognizant’s “gap from AI to impact,” Thoughtworks’ “next Tuesday.”
3. **Strategy and the tool were bought separately.** A deck from one firm, a CRM from another, and nobody connects them. Bluleadz says this in one sentence.
4. **The old platform is on a clock.** Praecipio puts Atlassian Data Center end of life, 28 March 2029, on the homepage. Migration is the concrete version of “disorganized data.”
5. **Work is fragmented, so agents are generic.** Fast Slow Motion and Publicis Sapient both say context is missing, so AI cannot act on how the business actually runs.
6. **Campaigns reset every quarter.** Weidert and IMPACT sell a system or a reputation, not another push. The tool is how the system keeps running.

## The biggest solutions

1. **Implementation as the product.** Configuration, migration, integration, training, and a managed service after go-live. Faye, Coastal, Praecipio, New Breed, SmartBug, and Aptitude 8 sell this directly.
2. **One firm for the motion and the tool.** Bluleadz will fix the strategy or the HubSpot system. Fast Slow Motion will do Salesforce, HubSpot, and Claude. The buyer does not want three vendors.
3. **A named method.** Endless Customers (IMPACT), the Growth Engine (Weidert), GTM Operating System (Bluleadz), FastShift (Adaptavist), VISTA (Praecipio), AI Pods (Globant). The method is what makes a services firm look repeatable.
4. **People who supervise agents.** Globant’s headline is the sharpest version. The rest of the set says the same thing more softly: certified humans, a dedicated pod (INSIDEA), former Salesforce executives (Coastal).
5. **Renew what they own.** Valtech’s “renew, not re-platform” and Coastal’s “do more with Salesforce and data.” The specialist does not ask the client to rip out the stack the last consultant installed.

## Fear, or solutions?

**Solutions-focused. None of the thirty lead with an attacker, a breach count, or a dollar loss.**

All 30 are scored solutions-focused in the CSV. A handful open on a problem, and the problem is a failed rollout or a stuck pilot, not a threat:

- Bluleadz: the CRM is duct tape.
- Wipro: billions in AI spend, still in pilot.
- Slalom: the gap between ambition and execution is widening.
- Praecipio: disorganized data will block the AI strategy, and Data Center has an end date. A later line, “Your competitors are getting smarter,” is the only competitive jab.

That is agitation in the service of a consultation. It is not the McAfee pattern from the vendor scan, where the next line is what the scam cost. The implementer does not need the buyer to be afraid. The buyer already owns software that is not paying off. The page has to sound like the crew that will finish the job.

## What the UX has in common

- **The commercial action is a conversation.** Talk, book, schedule, get in touch, contact. The vendor scan’s second button was often “watch a demo” of the product. Here there is no product demo, because the product belongs to someone else.
- **Proof is certifications and repetitions, not a free credit.** 1,200 HubSpot certifications, 750 Salesforce certifications, 8,400 projects, 4.99 from 500 reviews, Partner of the Year. The specialist borrows the vendor’s brand as the credential.
- **The platform is either in the first sentence or it is missing on purpose.** Fast Slow Motion, New Breed, SmartBug, Coastal, Praecipio, and Bluleadz name it immediately. Accenture, Infosys, EPAM, and Capgemini do not. Both are choices. Naming it catches the buyer who already picked HubSpot. Leaving it out catches the buyer who has not.
- **No checkout.** Price, plan grids, and free trials belong to the vendor study. The implementer homepage ends in a form or a calendar.
- **Carousels and newsrooms hide the offer** on the large sites. Accenture, Slalom, Adaptavist, Wipro, and Merkle make the visitor hunt. The specialists with the clearest first screen, Bluleadz, INSIDEA, Fast Slow Motion, New Breed, and Faye, put the outcome, the platform, and the button in one view.

## What to copy, if the firm is the one that shows up and installs it

The specialist homepages that match this job do four things the global integrators do not. They name the platform in the first two sentences. They show a partner tier or a review count in that same view. They describe the failure mode in the client’s language, duct tape, a deck that never shipped, a pilot that never scaled. And they offer one conversation, not a trial of someone else’s software.
