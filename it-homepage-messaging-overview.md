# What the top IT homepages are actually saying

Captured 7 October 2026. Thirty vendor homepages were read for the live headline, subheadline, and calls to action. The row-level record is in `it-homepage-messaging.csv`.

## How the thirty were chosen

“Performing well” here means two things at once: people already arrive, and the page asks them to do something commercial.

Traffic is taken from Similarweb’s global category rankings for September 2026 (published 1 October 2026) and from DataForSEO’s estimate of monthly Google traffic in the United States, organic and paid. Paid-search traffic is the public stand-in for conversion investment. Engagement (time on site, pages per visit, bounce rate) is included where Similarweb published it. A free trial, a demo, a credit, or a price on the page is the other conversion signal.

Login properties were left out even when they outrank everyone. In September 2026, `cloud.microsoft`, `office.com`, `duosecurity.com`, `force.com`, and `myworkday.com` are among the most visited addresses in these categories because they are the product, not the pitch. They do not have a marketing headline. GoDaddy is a top hosting property, but every fetch of the homepage returned 403, so Zendesk takes that seat: large US search traffic and a visible trial-or-demo choice.

The set covers cloud, enterprise software, developer tools, hardware, hosting, and security. It is not a single league table. Adobe’s US Google estimate is about 216 million visits a month. Hostinger’s is about 441,000, but Hostinger is 4th in Similarweb’s hosting category, holds visitors for almost 8 minutes, and spends heavily on paid search relative to its organic traffic. Both belong.

## The promise that is getting shared

The same sentence is traveling across the enterprise sites, with the nouns swapped.

**AI agents do the work, on your data, inside one platform, under your rules.**

- Salesforce: “Your agents, your team, and your data — working together.”
- ServiceNow: “AI doesn’t just think. It acts.”
- Azure: “Ship AI agents faster.”
- Okta, on the product slides behind an event recap: “Okta secures AI” and “Welcome to the secure agentic future.”
- CrowdStrike: “One unified platform to secure the agentic enterprise.”
- Cloudflare: “One platform for your apps, agents, and workforce.”
- HubSpot’s eyebrow is “Agentic Customer Platform,” and the headline is still the old trio: build demand, win deals, delight customers.
- Atlassian’s version is context: “the power of knowing,” so humans and agents decide from the same record.
- Zendesk puts a number on it: automate up to 80% of interactions.
- Hostinger brings the same idea down-market: “An agentic platform to build, run, and grow your business online.”

Hardware shares a different sentence: **this machine is built for local AI, and you can buy it now.** Microsoft’s live hero is Surface Laptop Ultra on an NVIDIA RTX Spark chip. NVIDIA’s first slide is that same PC. Dell pairs a discount with “Built for the AI Agent Era.” Lenovo says “Smarter AI for all.” Intel’s page title is “Built for AI,” and the first slide is a Googlebook on an Intel platform.

Consumer security shares a third sentence: **threats got smarter than antivirus, so one plan should handle scams, identity, and the device.** Norton says you should not have to think about it. McAfee says safe was antivirus and safer is everything else. Kaspersky says it stays a step ahead and keeps the device smooth.

What is not getting shared anymore is a feature list in the hero. The feature list has moved under the headline, into named modules: Agentforce, Foundry, Falcon, Teamwork Graph, the HubSpot hubs.

## Where the promise is delivered

The headline is the claim. The next screen is the proof. The button is how you enter.

**Enterprise software and cloud deliver the promise as a map, then a number.** Salesforce follows “#1 AI CRM” with AIforce, Agentforce, Customer 360, and Data 360, and a 34% productivity figure. ServiceNow follows “expensive advice” with Control Tower, Flow, and Otto, then 85% of the Fortune 500 and a 98% renewal rate. IBM puts a customer result on every product card (code fully refactored, HR cost down 40%, downtime down 47%). AWS states twenty years of leadership and then shows 124 availability zones. Cloudflare states “20% of the Internet” and then publishes the price: $0, $20, $200, or a contract.

**The commercial door is a pair of buttons.** One is self-serve and low commitment. The other is a conversation.

| Self-serve | Conversation |
| --- | --- |
| Start for free, Get started free, Try for free, Start building for free, $300 credit, Download Copilot, Pre-order, Shop now, Buy now | Watch demo, Get a demo, Request demo, Contact us, Free product demo, Book a briefing |

HubSpot, Zendesk, Atlassian, Salesforce, and ServiceNow use both. Cloudflare, Hostinger, Norton, McAfee, and Kaspersky put the price on the page and expect a checkout. CrowdStrike does both: explore the platform, and try Falcon for 15 days with no card.

**Hardware delivers the promise in the cart.** Dell’s first real action is “Shop now for deals.” Microsoft’s is “Pre-order now.” NVIDIA and Intel use “Learn more” because the buyer often purchases the chip inside someone else’s machine. Lenovo splits the path: Learn more for the vision, Sign up now for the business store, with $100 off.

**A few homepages deliver the promise somewhere other than a hero.** Fortinet’s standing line (“cybersecurity everywhere you need it”) sits in the title and the header. The page itself is awards, threat reports, and event registration, and the button in the nav is “Free product demo.” Okta’s first slide is an event recap (“Missed the livestream?”); the product headlines are the next two slides, while Free trial stays in the nav. Cisco leads with “Evolve your approach to cyber defense” and a WebexOne registration, and only then shows Cloud Control, AI Canvas, and the firewall. Autodesk’s homepage delivers a report, not the product. The button is “Get the report.” Oracle leads with AI World registration, then the cost claim.

**Proof is a short list, and it repeats.** Fortune 500 share (Cloudflare 45%, Cisco 99%, ServiceNow 85%). A single percentage (Zendesk 80%, Salesforce 34%, Palo Alto 90% lower MTTR). An analyst badge, mostly Gartner or Forrester, on CrowdStrike, Fortinet, Palo Alto, Zendesk, and GitHub. A customer quote with a name, on IBM, Cloudflare, and Zoho.

## The pain points being solved

Six pains account for almost every hero.

1. **AI that does not do the work.** ServiceNow says it plainly: most companies have AI, and few have the data, the workflow, and the control to let it act. IBM aims the same complaint at old, mission-critical software.
2. **Tools and data that do not share a context.** HubSpot, Salesforce, Atlassian, Zoho, and CrowdStrike all describe teams, agents, and records living apart. The buyer is not short of software. The buyer cannot get one answer.
3. **Agents as a new thing that has to be governed.** Okta treats the agent as another identity. CrowdStrike treats it as another thing that can be breached. Azure and Cloudflare treat it as another workload to ship and secure.
4. **Work that does not scale with headcount.** Zendesk (80% of interactions), HubSpot (demand, deals, service), and GitHub (security fixes that steal time from building) are the same pain in three departments.
5. **Infrastructure that is slow, expensive, or split.** AWS, Google Cloud, Oracle, and Cloudflare sell speed, credits, and one network. Oracle adds a specific pain: AI capacity is scarce, and the database should run in whichever cloud you already use.
6. **Harm the buyer cannot see.** This is the security pain, and it splits. Enterprise pages talk about a spread-out attack surface and breaches. Consumer pages talk about scams, phishing, and identity theft, with a dollar figure attached.

## The biggest solutions

Five offers are doing the work.

1. **One platform for humans and agents.** This is the dominant enterprise offer: Salesforce, ServiceNow, HubSpot, Atlassian, Zoho, CrowdStrike, Cloudflare, Cisco. The mechanism is shared data plus permissions, not a smarter chatbot.
2. **A free start.** Credits (Google Cloud’s $300), a free build (Cloudflare, Hostinger), a free CRM (HubSpot), a free trial (Adobe, Zendesk, Salesforce, CrowdStrike, Okta, IBM), or a download (GitHub Copilot). The sites with the best engagement in this set, AWS, Azure, Zoho, and Hostinger, all let a visitor go deep before talking to sales.
3. **A security platform that replaces a pile of tools.** CrowdStrike’s “collect once and use everywhere,” Fortinet’s fabric, Palo Alto’s platformization, Bitdefender’s three doors for home, small business, and enterprise.
4. **One consumer plan for scam, device, and identity.** Norton, McAfee, and Kaspersky. The plan card is the product. Coverage amounts ($1 million, $2 million) and a virus-protection promise are the guarantee.
5. **A PC or a cloud built to run AI locally or cheaply.** Surface and RTX Spark, Dell and Lenovo Copilot+ PCs, NVIDIA’s data-center ROI story, Oracle’s capacity build-out, Azure’s Foundry.

## Fear, or solutions?

**Solutions-focused. Fear is a specialist tactic, not the industry default.**

Of these 30 homepages, 25 open on an outcome: ship, build, automate, know, create, modernize, start. Four are hybrid: they name a real threat and then sell the platform or the plan. One, McAfee, builds the argument on loss.

| Tone | Sites | How it shows up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solutions-focused (25) | Microsoft, GitHub, Adobe, AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, Salesforce, IBM, Oracle, Cloudflare, NVIDIA, Dell, Lenovo, Cisco, Intel, Atlassian, Zoho, Autodesk, Hostinger, Zendesk, CrowdStrike, Okta, Bitdefender, HubSpot, ServiceNow | The headline is a result. Threats are absent, or they are the thing the product stops, without a statistic of harm. |
| Hybrid (4) | Palo Alto Networks, Fortinet, Norton, Kaspersky | Palo Alto prints the increases: exploited zero-days, ransomware, breaches, then answers with platformization. Fortinet’s page is awards plus reports about AI speeding up attacks. Norton leads with ease, then cites opened phishing emails and 130 million Americans hit by cybercrime. Kaspersky leads with “a step ahead,” then 5.3 billion attacks detected. |
| Fear-based (1) | McAfee | “Safer is everything” is a reframe, but the next lines are the sale: scams cost 1 in 3 Americans $1,160 on average, and 3,322 breaches happened at companies you use. |

Enterprise security that is winning attention is not scaring people in the hero. CrowdStrike’s title is “We stop breaches,” and the page underneath is a platform, a 15-day trial, and analyst badges. Cloudflare never mentions an attacker in the hero. Bitdefender says “global leader” and asks you to pick a door. The fear frame survives in consumer subscriptions, where the conversion is a credit card on the same page, and on Palo Alto’s homepage, where the bounce rate is the highest of the enterprise security sites in this set (53.81%).

ServiceNow is the useful contrast. It opens on a problem, “AI without workflows is just expensive advice,” and that problem is wasted money, not an enemy. Problem-first and fear-based are not the same move.

## What the UX has in common

- **One sentence of outcome, one sentence of mechanism.** The headline is the result. The subheadline says who it is for or how it works. Salesforce, HubSpot, Cloudflare, Zendesk, Atlassian, and Hostinger all do this cleanly.
- **A number within one scroll.** If the headline will not carry a statistic, the next band will: 80%, 20% of the internet, 306,000 customers, $300, 15 days.
- **Two speeds of commitment.** Free or shop on one side, demo or contact on the other. Sites that only offer “Learn more” (NVIDIA’s carousel, Intel’s carousel, parts of Cisco) are brand and product browsers, not checkout pages.
- **Carousels dilute the promise.** Microsoft, NVIDIA, Dell, Lenovo, Intel, and Okta rotate the hero. The visitor does not get one sentence. Okta’s product story is real and it is not the first slide.
- **A chat is becoming a third CTA.** Dell asks “How can I help you today?” AWS turns the free trial into “I want to try AWS for free.” Azure, Cisco, and Microsoft offer an assistant beside the buttons.
- **Price on the page marks a self-serve business.** Cloudflare, Hostinger, Norton, McAfee, Kaspersky, and CrowdStrike’s bundles. Everyone else hides price behind a demo.

## What to copy, if the goal is the same result

The pages that combine heavy traffic with a clear conversion ask do four things in the first screen: they name the outcome in the headline, they name the mechanism in the line under it, they offer a free or cheap start, and they put one proof number next to that offer. They talk about agents as labor, not as magic. They treat fear as a consumer-security tool, and they leave it there.
